The net force on a body equals the rate of change of the body's momentum. For a
body of constant mass this relation reduces to F = ma, where a is the
acceleration of the body.

The acceleration of a body is parallel to the net force acting on it and
inversely proportional to the mass of the body.

The total momentum of a closed system remains constant. A collision between
bodies within such a system redistributes momentum among them without altering
the total.

The momentum of a body is the product of its mass and its velocity, written
p = mv.

The kinetic energy of a body moving slowly compared with light is KE = mv^2/2,
proportional to the square of the speed of the body.

The gravitational force between two masses is proportional to the product of the
masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance separating their
centres. Its magnitude is F = G m1 m2 / r^2, where G is the gravitational
constant.

The electrostatic force between two point charges has magnitude
F = k q1 q2 / r^2 and acts along the line joining the charges. Like charges repel
and unlike charges attract.

The electric flux through a closed surface depends only on the charge enclosed
by that surface. Gauss's law expresses this as flux = Q/e0, where e0 is the
permittivity of free space.

A changing magnetic flux through a circuit induces an electromotive force in
that circuit. The induced electromotive force is EMF = -dPhi/dt, and the
negative sign indicates that the induced current opposes the change producing
it.

The Lorentz force on a charge q travelling with velocity v through electric and
magnetic fields is F = q(E + v x B). The magnetic contribution is perpendicular
to both the velocity of the charge and the magnetic field.

The speed of an electromagnetic wave in vacuum is c = 1/sqrt(mu0 e0), fixed by
the permeability and permittivity of free space and independent of the motion of
the source.

The energy of a photon is proportional to its frequency, written E = hf, where h
is the Planck constant.

The wavelength associated with a particle of momentum p is lambda = h/p. De
Broglie proposed this relation, which predicts the diffraction of electrons
through a crystal lattice.

The product of the uncertainties in the position and the momentum of a particle
satisfies dx dp >= hbar/2, where hbar is the reduced Planck constant.

The electrical resistance of a conductor relates the voltage across it to the
current through it. Ohm's law states this as V = IR.

The power dissipated by a resistor carrying current I at voltage V is P = IV.
