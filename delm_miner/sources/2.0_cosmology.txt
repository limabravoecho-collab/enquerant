The rest energy of a body is proportional to its mass, written E = mc^2, where c
is the speed of light in vacuum.

The Lorentz factor for a relative speed v is gamma = 1/sqrt(1 - v^2/c^2). It
approaches unity at low speed and diverges as the speed approaches that of
light.

A clock moving relative to an observer runs slow by the Lorentz factor as
measured in the frame the clock moves through.

The recession speed of a distant galaxy is proportional to its distance from the
observer. Hubble's law expresses this as v = H0 d, where H0 is the Hubble
constant and d the proper distance.

The Schwarzschild radius of a body of mass M is rs = 2GM/c^2. A body compressed
within this radius forms a black hole from which no signal escapes.

The expansion rate of a flat homogeneous universe is set by its total energy
density. The Friedmann equation gives this as H^2 = 8 pi G rho / 3, where rho is
the total density.

The critical density of the universe is rho_c = 3H^2/(8 pi G). A universe at
exactly this density is spatially flat.

The equation of state parameter of a component is the ratio of its pressure to
its energy density, written w = P/rho. A cosmological constant has w = -1.

The cosmic microwave background has the spectrum of a black body at
T = 2.725 K. This radiation is uniform across the sky to within one part in a
hundred thousand.

The power radiated per unit area by a black body depends only on its absolute
temperature. Stefan and Boltzmann expressed this as P = sigma T^4, where sigma
is the Stefan-Boltzmann constant.

The wavelength at which a black body radiates most strongly is inversely
proportional to its absolute temperature. Wien's displacement law states this as
lambda_max T = b.

The escape velocity from a spherical body of mass M at radius r is
v = sqrt(2GM/r). A projectile launched at this speed does not return.

The orbital period of a planet is related to the semi-major axis of its orbit.
Kepler's third law states that T^2 is proportional to a^3.
