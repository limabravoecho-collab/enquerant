The internal energy of a closed system changes by the heat added to the system
minus the work the system performs on its surroundings. This relation is written
dU = Q - W and is known as the first law of thermodynamics.

The total energy of an isolated system remains constant. No process occurring
within the boundary of an isolated system creates or destroys energy.

The entropy of an isolated system never decreases. For any spontaneous process
occurring within it, the entropy change satisfies dS >= 0.

The entropy change of a reversible process equals the heat transferred divided
by the absolute temperature at which the transfer occurs, written dS = dQ/T.

The entropy of a macrostate depends on the number of microscopic arrangements
consistent with it. Boltzmann expressed this as S = k ln W, where W counts those
arrangements and k is the Boltzmann constant.

The maximum efficiency of a heat engine operating between two thermal reservoirs
is eta = 1 - Tc/Th, where Th is the absolute temperature of the hot reservoir and
Tc that of the cold reservoir.

The pressure, volume, amount and absolute temperature of an ideal gas are
related by PV = nRT, where R is the universal gas constant.

The Gibbs free energy of a system is defined as G = H - TS, combining enthalpy,
absolute temperature and entropy.

A process occurring at constant temperature and pressure proceeds spontaneously
when the change in Gibbs free energy is negative.

Thermal equilibrium is transitive. Two systems each in thermal equilibrium with
a third system are in thermal equilibrium with one another.

The erasure of one bit of information in a system at absolute temperature T
dissipates at least dQ = k T ln 2 of heat. Landauer derived this lower bound,
which links information processing to thermodynamic cost.

The heat capacity of a substance is the heat required to raise its temperature
by one degree. For a substance of mass m and specific heat capacity c, the heat
absorbed is Q = m c dT.
